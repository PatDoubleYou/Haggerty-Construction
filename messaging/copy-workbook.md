## 2. Raw material (our words, lightly cleaned)

Keep this section. It's the source every draft pulls from.

### Where "smooth is fast" comes from
- Dad would remind us all the time. Earliest memory: cabinet installs for a
  whole townhouse, learning with him. Go too fast and you hang something
  wrong, drop a cabinet, make some big mistake. Take your time, think it
  through, do it right.
- Rushing to put finishing touches on a closet, stood on a shelf instead of
  grabbing a stepladder. It snapped in half.
- Not sure where Dad picked up the saying — maybe the Guard. **Ask him.**

### What a smooth job looked like (the ~$150K whole-home refresh, just finished)
- Laundry room, new cabinet doors and drawers, full master bathroom, kitchen
  cabinets redone. "Big for us, and also small in a lot of ways."
- They got an actual contract. "Might not be perfect but it's something" —
  it came in handy a few times.
- They got a pre-construction agreement. Nobody else we know does that for
  remodels.
- Pre-construction = take the time up front: selections, risks, what can go
  wrong, a realistic schedule, budget dialed in close. *Then* launch.
- Finished on time, except changes the clients made themselves — and those
  were all documented.
- Closeout: punch list + an "open commitments" list. A countertop issue took
  a couple of months; we delivered it.

### The horror story we're the answer to
- Remodels still not finished years later. Contractors take forever.
- Cheap bid wins → change order, change order, change order.
- Nobody talked about what could happen, or what finish level they wanted.
  "Oh you can choose that, but it'll do this and that" — but we're already
  mid-project.
- Lead times blow up the schedule. Waiting six weeks for cabinets because
  you thought you could order them in a couple of days.
- Fast bathroom companies: what do they do when they hit mold? Or anything
  beyond a standard tub-liner swap? You're stuck.

### Why people pick us over a cheaper bid (our guess — confirm with clients)
- They like us. We're hungry, respectful, we care. The project means a lot
  to us and so does our reputation here.
- Not faceless — a family company making a name for ourselves. Shows in
  our communication and rapport.
- "We aren't salespeople, but we know how to sell a remodel."

### Blake
- Clients notice how he communicates and how organized he is.
- Great at pulling a schedule together; talks to clients and subs equally
  well; educates clients when needed; keeps everyone updated.
- No problem having tough conversations. Holds people accountable both ways
  — client and subs.
- Mediates and orchestrates complex remodels.
- Once put sticky notes on the walls with the actual scope, plus a list of
  those notes for the electrician, so every detail was handled even when he
  couldn't be on site.

### What we won't do
- Start a big project without a contract.
- Start a big remodel without pre-construction — even if selections are
  already made. Document everything; when do we need to choose things and
  why; how does it hit schedule and budget. (Small jobs can be simpler.)

### How the process actually works
1. **Detailed ballpark estimate** — free. Haggerty OS lets us turn it
   around fast (usually same/next day — *don't promise that publicly*).
2. **Pre-construction** — paid, about **3% down** (priced off the estimate),
   **credited toward the build** if they go forward.
   Filters out tire-kickers. Signed via the Pre-Construction Service
   Agreement.
3. **Project Success Plan** — the deliverable: scope, overview, budget,
   selections, schedule — the whole plan. **The client keeps it, no
   obligation.**
4. **Construction contract** — then we build.

> "There's no obligation… but why wouldn't you go forward with us?"

### How it works (new — the four steps)

> **1. Free detailed estimate.** We walk your space and send a real,
> line-item estimate — usually fast.
>
> **2. Pre-construction.** For about 3% of your estimate — credited toward

> what plans are supposed to do.

Why this section matters for conversions: it tells a visitor exactly what
happens after they click, puts a price on the first real commitment, and
"yours to keep" removes the fear of getting locked in. The existing
"Example Estimate + Project Success Plan" download is the proof — link it
right here.

> **A real contract.** Every big job starts with one. It's come in handy

> **Lead times planned in.** If your cabinets take six weeks, we know before
