The usual way

You've probably heard the other story.

Somebody takes the cheap bid. Then it's change order, change order, change order. Nobody asked what finish level they wanted. Nobody mentioned the cabinets take six weeks. Some of those remodels still aren't finished years later.

Usually the problem isn't bad work. It's that nobody made a plan.

The promise

What "smooth" actually looks like.

  • A real contract

    Every big job starts with one. It's come in handy more than once.

  • Lead times planned in

    If your cabinets take six weeks, we know before demo, not halfway through.

  • Changes in writing

    Want to change something mid-project? You'll see what it does to the schedule and budget before it happens.

  • One person running it

    Blake builds the schedule, keeps you and every sub updated, and has the tough conversations early. On one job he stuck notes on the walls marking the exact scope for the electrician, so nothing got missed on the days he couldn't be there.

  • A real closeout

    A punch list, plus a written list of anything still open. On our last big remodel, a countertop issue took a couple of months to sort out. It stayed on the list until we delivered.

Why we work this way

Smooth is fast.

Dad has a saying: slow is smooth, smooth is fast. I first heard it hanging cabinets with him in a townhouse when I was learning the trade. Rush it and you hang one wrong, or drop it, and now you're slower than if you'd just taken your time.

I've relearned it the hard way. Once, rushing to finish a closet, I stood on a shelf instead of grabbing a stepladder and snapped it clean in half.

That's how we run remodels now. Take your time, think it through, do it right.
